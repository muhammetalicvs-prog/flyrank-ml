Research overview

Abstract

FlyRank's practical content problem is deciding which pages should be reviewed first when editors and SEO reviewers cannot inspect every content record.

Using 30,000 anonymized content-level records, this project compared an interpretable fixed-rule baseline with Logistic Regression and Random Forest on a client-grouped holdout split. Precision@50 was selected as the primary metric because the intended output is a short human-review queue rather than an automated content-editing system.

The fixed-rule baseline achieved a Precision@50 of 0.88, compared with 0.64 for Logistic Regression and 0.58 for Random Forest. This means that 44 of the first 50 recommendations produced by the baseline were positive cases. Although Random Forest achieved the highest ROC-AUC at 0.6111, it did not produce the strongest first 50 recommendations.

The results support using the transparent fixed-rule baseline as the initial content-review queue. However, this was an offline evaluation and does not prove that reviewing or editing the recommended pages will improve traffic, engagement, or business performance.

The decision being supported

Introduction / Problem Statement

FlyRank content teams may have more pages to review than editors and SEO specialists can inspect manually. The practical problem is therefore not whether every page should be changed, but which content records should be reviewed first.

This project treats the task as a ranking and decision-support problem. The system does not automatically edit, remove, or publish content. Instead, it produces a short and explainable queue that helps human reviewers focus on records with stronger evidence of content-review opportunity.

The study compares a transparent fixed-rule CTR baseline with Logistic Regression and Random Forest. The goal is to determine whether additional machine learning complexity improves the first 50 recommendations that a reviewer would realistically inspect.

Research question: Can historical search visibility, click-through, engagement, and content-freshness signals help FlyRank identify which content records should be reviewed first?

Decision supported

The output supports editorial and SEO prioritization by placing potentially useful candidates near the top of a limited review queue.

Human role

A reviewer still decides whether a page requires a title, metadata, content, engagement, or freshness intervention.

Public-safe evidence

Data

The project uses the public-safe, anonymized dataset supplied through the FlyRank ML Internship. The release was derived from approximately 79 million rows of production search and analytics data.

Modeling and ranking used the prepared content_refresh_anonymized.csv table containing 30,000 content-level records and 44 columns. Signals include impressions, clicks, click-through rate, average search position, engagement measures, content freshness, and historical trend information.

Public-safety and leakage exclusions

Client and content identifiers were retained only for grouping or traceability and were not used as model features. Current-period and previous-period fields used to construct the label were excluded from the model frame.

Direct label sources, including trend_direction and trend_pct, were also excluded because they directly reveal or closely approximate the target.

Public-safe publication: No client names, page URLs, private search queries, credentials, or identifying production records are displayed on this page.

How the comparison was made

Methodology

Target and assumptions

The target is a binary decline indicator derived from a future outcome period. A positive label identifies a record that declined under the project's prepared definition.

Historical signals are treated as prioritization evidence, not as proof that editing a page will cause improvement.

Baseline and models

The Week 4 baseline combines meaningful search visibility, position, weak click-through performance, and related opportunity signals into an interpretable action score.

It was compared with probability rankings from Logistic Regression and Random Forest.

Validation design

The split was grouped by client rather than created through random row sampling.

Training contained 23,837 records from 25 clients. Testing contained 6,163 records from 7 different clients, with zero client overlap.

Evaluation

Precision@50 is the primary operational metric because a reviewer is expected to inspect a small queue rather than every record in the test set.

Precision@20, Recall@50, Average Precision, ROC-AUC, and the test-set base rate are included as supporting measures.

The baseline's Precision@50 of 0.88 means that 44 of its first 50 recommendations were positive cases on the held-out test set. Logistic Regression reached 0.64 and Random Forest reached 0.58.

Random Forest's higher ROC-AUC reflects broader discrimination across the full test set, but it did not create the strongest small review queue. A more complex model was therefore not automatically the best choice for the operational objective.

Decision: Retain the fixed-rule baseline as the initial queue generator for this dataset, label definition, validation split, and review capacity.

What the evidence cannot prove

Limitations & Honest Framing

This is an offline evaluation. It does not measure what happens after editors act and does not prove that changing a recommended page will improve traffic, engagement, conversions, or other business outcomes.

The label is a proxy for future decline rather than a direct measure of refresh value. Seasonality, changes in search demand, competition, SERP features, technical problems, and other unobserved factors may influence the outcome.

The fixed-rule weights and thresholds were manually designed. The machine learning models were not exhaustively tuned, and the findings come from one prepared dataset and one client-grouped holdout.

Performance may differ across future datasets, client groups, markets, time periods, labels, and review capacities.

Important: A high queue precision identifies records associated with the project label. It does not establish that a specific editorial intervention will cause a positive result.

From ranking to action

Ranked Recommendations

1

Review title and meta information

Prioritize pages with meaningful visibility but weak click-through. Compare the title and description with likely search intent, the page promise, and competing SERP features.

2

Refresh visible but aging content

Inspect older pages that still receive impressions. Verify dates, examples, screenshots, and whether newer competing content answers the need more effectively.

3

Investigate engagement weakness

For records with visibility or clicks but weak engagement, review content-message alignment, readability, internal links, calls to action, performance, and layout.

4

Reassess low-confidence candidates

Keep low-volume and borderline records lower in the queue. Gather more observations and consider seasonality and intervention cost before acting.

Operational next step: Review the top 50 records, document the action and outcome, and use those post-review observations to recalibrate thresholds and improve future labels.
